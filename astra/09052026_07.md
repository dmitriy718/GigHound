Temporary log paths identify evidence from this session, not durable release artifacts. Committed regression sources, the repeatable UI runner and the recorded results make the checks reproducible after temporary files disappear.

## Remaining work and release gates

The audit is **not represented as 100% closed**. Production load qualification, further large-view decomposition, accounting-grade replacement of legacy Float money storage, and a self-service verified-email journey remain engineering/product opportunities or deployment gates. The audited unbounded list paths and cross-process adapter pacing have now been addressed. Structured multi-package browser editing and interactive remote-browser takeover are explicitly unavailable; the manual paths contain their original failure modes. These limitations have been documented, not silently assumed accepted by the user.

Before production rollout, the operator must reconcile historical duplicate/unbound/unsnapshotted work, retain a real backup/key pair, configure per-worker secrets and network egress boundaries, verify provider permission and account capabilities, and run a controlled canary. The local synthetic fixtures cannot supply evidence about real customer data, provider authorization, or platform selector compatibility.

The ten suggested features have first-release implementations (see `_06.md` and `docs/operations.md`). Their larger predictive, onboarding, shared-account dispatch and causal-lift ambitions remain experiments with separate success criteria; no exponential value or revenue increase is claimed.


## Final proof notes

- The first single-tenant index fixture used the existing status index plus a sort, completing in approximately 0.12 ms. That was valid optimizer behavior, not evidence that the compound index was used. A separate 10,000-job/100-tenant fixture demonstrated natural selection of the compound tenant/status/time index. No forced planner setting or general speedup claim was used.
- Final UI verification exposed and repaired the empty-analysis crash (A065). The test runner now owns process groups, checks its ports before launch, and cleans up its temporary API/Vite servers and database on success or failure.
- Final preflight on the disposable database reported no duplicates, legacy approvals or unbound pending/claimed tasks. This says nothing about the user's live historical data, which was not mutated during implementation.
- Containers used for tests and recovery drills are disposable and separate from the running Gighound stack. Verification images remain local; no image was pushed and no deployment was performed.

Final image/runtime check: the pinned non-root worker image launched Chromium and preserved Unicode text. Disposable API, PostgreSQL, Redis/AOF test containers, the test network and the named persistence-test volume were removed after verification. The user's running Gighound containers were not stopped or replaced.
